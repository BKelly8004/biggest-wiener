The Waiver Wire Special

Charred Whole Dog · Corn Tortilla · Chipotle Emulsion

Pairs with a can of Busch Light, room temperature, from the twelve-pack nobody claimed at the draft party

I scored a hot dog in a tight crosshatch and grilled it whole over screaming heat because sometimes the only way to get something to perform is to apply pressure it never consented to — a philosophy I discovered too late applies to fantasy rosters, not just meat. I paid $53 to keep Malik Nabers over the offseason and started him in the season opener like a man who'd thought this through. He put up 9.60. My flex spot, an $11 rental named Xavier Worthy, put up a flat 0.00 — a number that takes some doing, and he found it in his very first game for me. None of it mattered in week one, when McGlynn's team put up 89.92 to my 82.96 and I found out in real time what the next fourteen weeks were going to feel like. He'd do it again in week twelve. I did not learn anything in between.
ProteinAll-beef hot dogs
MethodGrill, crosshatch char
VesselGF corn tortillas
Time15 minutes
Ingredients — Serves 2
  • 4 all-beef hot dogs, left whole
  • 6 GF corn tortillas
  • ½ cup mayonnaise + 1–2 chipotles in adobo, blended smooth
  • 1½ cups green cabbage, very thinly shaved
  • ¼ cup pickled jalapeños
  • 2 oz cotija cheese, crumbled
  • 1 lime, cut into wedges
  • Neutral oil, kosher salt
Preparation
01Heat a grill or cast iron skillet over high heat until smoking. Add a thin film of neutral oil if using a pan.
02Score each hot dog in a tight crosshatch pattern, cutting about ⅛-inch deep on all sides. This lets the fat render out and the surface curl and crisp as it cooks.
03Grill whole, turning every 60–90 seconds, until deeply charred and the scored cuts have opened up and blistered — about 6–8 minutes total.
04Warm tortillas directly over a gas flame or dry pan until lightly charred at the edges.
05Spread chipotle mayo on each tortilla. Lay one whole charred dog in each. Top with cabbage, jalapeños, cotija. Finish with an aggressive squeeze of lime and flaky salt.
My Note

Leave it whole. A hot dog that isn't being firmly handled — held together, scored with intent, put through real heat — is just lying there doing nothing, much like my roster management from weeks one through thirteen. Commit to the char. The crust does not form from hesitation.

Pigs in a Playoff Blanket

Upside-Down Puff Pastry Dog · Gruyère · Everything Crust

Pairs with Two-Buck Chuck, the red, until the color in the glass and the color coming back up are the same shade

Wrapped in pastry, flipped upside down, baked until the cheese underneath crisps into something lacquered and completely disconnected from what's actually inside. This dog made it to the postseason and is handling it better than I ever would have. I scored 1,327 points across the season — dead last in a twelve-team league, about 300 behind the champion — which is not a slump, it's a personality. The pastry only has to hold together for twenty minutes. I had four months.
ProteinAll-beef hot dogs
MethodOven bake, inversion
VesselGF puff pastry
Time10 min + 20 min oven
Ingredients — Serves 2
  • 4 all-beef hot dogs
  • 1 sheet GF puff pastry, thawed — verify label; Pepperidge Farm is NOT GF
  • 4 slices Gruyère or Swiss, cut to fit
  • 1 egg, beaten
  • 2 tbsp everything bagel seasoning
  • Whole grain mustard, for serving
  • Parchment paper
Preparation
01Preheat oven to 400°F. Line a baking sheet with parchment and lightly grease it.
02Lay cheese slices directly on the parchment. Place one hot dog centered on top of each.
03Cut puff pastry into strips wide enough to wrap each dog with slight overlap. Drape over, tucking edges beneath. Brush generously with egg wash. Press everything bagel seasoning firmly into the surface.
04Bake 18–22 minutes until deeply golden and the cheese has crisped and caramelized at the visible edges. The color should make you slightly nervous before you pull it.
05Rest exactly 3 minutes. Slide a thin spatula fully underneath and flip cheese-side up onto a board. Serve immediately with whole grain mustard.
My Note

I dropped Dak Prescott for Jared Goff in week nine with the quiet confidence of a man who has learned nothing. The pastry flip requires the same energy — commitment, no hesitation, accepting what's underneath. Unlike that decision, this one worked out.

Tartare of Last Resort

All-Beef Hot Dog · Capers · Dijon · Shallot · Raw Egg Yolk

Pairs with a shot of Malort, taken the way you take bad news — fast, and without asking anyone to explain it first

Finely diced, plated with a raw yolk on top like it's trying to seduce someone at a wedding. It's a hot dog wearing a tuxedo it stole. Ring mold, capers, shallot, dijon, the whole classical French treatment applied to $53 worth of processed pork that lasted exactly as long as Malik Nabers did on my roster — three weeks, gone before anyone looked too closely. I could say I served it with a straight face and zero regrets, the same energy I brought to every start/sit decision that ruined my season. Realistically, it went down easier than any of those did — though both are probably headed for the same regretful outcome.
ProteinAll-beef hot dogs
MethodNo-cook · knife work
VesselRing mold, plated
Time15 minutes
Ingredients — Serves 2
  • 4 all-beef hot dogs, very finely diced — knife work only, no food processor
  • 2 tbsp shallot, minced
  • 1 tbsp capers, roughly chopped
  • 1 tsp dijon mustard
  • 1 tsp Worcestershire sauce ⚠️ GF (Lea & Perrins is GF)
  • ½ tsp hot sauce
  • 2 raw egg yolks
  • Cornichons and GF crackers or cucumber rounds, to serve
  • Flaky salt, black pepper, chives to finish
Preparation
01Dice hot dogs as finely as possible — small, uniform pieces. This is not the time for rustic. Treat it like it deserves to be here.
02In a bowl, combine diced hot dog, shallot, capers, dijon, Worcestershire, and hot sauce. Mix gently until just combined. Season with salt and pepper. Taste. Adjust.
03Press mixture firmly into a ring mold on each plate. Smooth the top. Carefully unmold.
04Nestle one raw egg yolk into a small indent pressed into the top of each tartare. It should sit there with quiet confidence.
05Finish with chives and flaky salt. Arrange cornichons and crackers or cucumber alongside. Serve immediately and accept the compliments without revealing what it is until after the first bite.
My Note

The knife work is the entire point. A food processor turns this into paste and the illusion collapses entirely — much like my season once people looked at the actual standings. Fine dice. Steady hand. Committed presentation. The confidence has to come from somewhere since the ingredient isn't providing it.

Fourth and Wellington

Hot Dog Wellington · Mushroom Duxelles · Prosciutto · GF Puff Pastry

Pairs with whatever's left in the fridge after a 61-point loss, served at the temperature of a locker room an hour after the game ended

Forty-five minutes of prep to wrap a hot dog in prosciutto, mushrooms, and pastry, and it came out of the oven better than any decision I made between September and December. In week four Trevor put 158.82 on me, I lost by 61.04, and my response was to drop Lamar Jackson. A former MVP, cut loose the same week I got run out of the building by the team that went on to win the whole thing. I picked up Dak Prescott like a man who had learned something. I hadn't — he lasted five weeks before Jared Goff took his job too. The pastry shatters. The inside is exactly what it said it would be, which makes it the only thing all year that was.
ProteinAll-beef hot dogs
MethodDuxelles wrap, oven roast
VesselGF puff pastry
Time45 min prep · 25 min oven
Ingredients — Serves 2
  • 4 all-beef hot dogs, patted very dry
  • 8 oz cremini or baby bella mushrooms, very finely chopped
  • 2 tbsp butter
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tsp fresh thyme leaves
  • 4 slices prosciutto, laid flat
  • 1 sheet GF puff pastry, thawed ⚠️ verify GF label
  • 1 egg, beaten (egg wash)
  • Dijon mustard, for brushing
  • Flaky salt, black pepper
  • Whole grain mustard, to serve
Preparation
01Make the duxelles: melt butter in a skillet over medium-high heat. Add mushrooms and cook, stirring often, until all moisture has evaporated and the mixture is dark, dry, and deeply concentrated — about 12–15 minutes. Add garlic and thyme for the final 2 minutes. Season and cool completely.
02Brush each hot dog lightly with dijon. Lay a sheet of plastic wrap flat. Arrange 2 prosciutto slices overlapping in a rectangle. Spread a thin, even layer of cooled duxelles over the prosciutto. Place a hot dog at the edge and roll tightly using the plastic wrap to help. Twist the ends to seal. Refrigerate 15 minutes minimum.
03Preheat oven to 425°F. Cut puff pastry into rectangles large enough to wrap each prosciutto-wrapped dog. Remove plastic wrap, place the roll at the pastry edge, roll tightly, and seal the seam and ends with egg wash. Brush the entire surface with egg wash.
04Score the pastry top lightly with a knife in a crosshatch or diagonal pattern. Sprinkle with flaky salt. Place seam-side down on a parchment-lined baking sheet.
05Bake 20–25 minutes until the pastry is deeply golden and shatteringly crisp. Rest 5 minutes before slicing. Serve with whole grain mustard and the quiet satisfaction of having done something correctly for once.
My Note

The duxelles must be completely dry before you use them. Any residual moisture turns the pastry soggy from the inside — everything looks right until you cut in and find the middle gave out. Cook the mushrooms longer than feels necessary. The moment you decide they're done is about ten minutes before they actually are, which is also roughly when I decided I was done with Lamar Jackson.

The Cannelloni Consolation

Bratwurst-Stuffed Cannelloni · Sun-Dried Tomato Cream · Baked

Pairs with a warm Natty Light found under the couch, consolation bracket rules apply to beverages too

The sauce alone has ended engagements in the good direction. I stuffed a whole bratwurst inside a cannelloni tube, baked it in that same sun-dried tomato cream until the pasta went tender and the brat cooked through, then sliced it down the middle so the cross-section does the talking. Nothing else I made this year survived being opened up and looked at. I finished with the fewest points in the entire league — not the fewest wins, the fewest points. Five teams had worse records than me and still outscored me. That's not variance, that's a signature.
ProteinBratwurst
MethodStuff, bake in sauce
VesselGF cannelloni tubes
Time6 min sear + 35 min bake
Ingredients — Serves 2
  • 4 bratwurst links, casings left on
  • 8 GF cannelloni tubes (par-boiled 3 minutes to soften, drained)
  • 4 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
  • ½ cup sun-dried tomatoes in oil, roughly chopped
  • 1½ cups heavy cream
  • ¾ cup parmesan, finely grated, plus more to finish
  • 1 tsp red pepper flakes
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • Fresh basil, to finish
  • Olive oil, salt, black pepper
Preparation
01Preheat oven to 375°F. Par-boil cannelloni tubes in salted water for 3 minutes — just enough to soften, not fully cook. Drain and lay flat on a towel.
02Sear bratwurst whole in a hot pan with no oil, turning until deeply browned on all sides, about 6 minutes total. They won't be cooked through — that finishes in the oven.
03Once cool enough to handle, carefully slide one seared brat into each softened cannelloni tube. The casing should hold everything together.
04In the same pan, add olive oil, garlic, sun-dried tomatoes, red pepper flakes, and oregano. Sauté 1 minute. Pour in cream, simmer 3–4 minutes until slightly thickened, then stir in parmesan until melted. Season aggressively.
05Spread a thin layer of sauce in the bottom of a baking dish. Arrange stuffed cannelloni in a single layer. Pour remaining sauce over the top, making sure every tube is coated. Cover with foil and bake 25 minutes, then uncover and bake 10 more minutes until bubbling and golden at the edges.
06Rest 5 minutes. Slice each cannelloni in half lengthwise on the plate to reveal the brat inside. Finish with extra parmesan and torn basil.
My Note

Don't skip the par-boil — a fully raw tube won't soften enough in the bake time and you'll be cutting into something closer to a breadstick than a pasta. Half-cooked going in is the move. I have a well-documented history of not preparing things properly before committing to them. The pasta doesn't get that same excuse.

The Ratfuck

Grilled All-Beef Dog · Full Elote Treatment · Charred Corn

Pairs with Milwaukee's Best, drunk with the same commitment you gave your flex spot all season — none

Scored, charred, and dressed up in mayo, cotija, and lime. The corn gets the same treatment and the corn earned it. Week fourteen I beat JP by 2.02 and felt something dangerously close to competent — the last game of the regular season, going out on a win that didn't mean anything since I'd already missed the playoffs. Week fifteen, same opponent, first round of the consolation bracket, I lost by 15.74 — the fastest I've ever gone from cautiously optimistic to face-down in a ditch. Whatever's working stops the second I trust it. Ratfucked by my own optimism, one week apart.
ProteinAll-beef hot dogs
MethodGrill or cast iron char
VesselStandalone — no bun
Time20 minutes
Ingredients — Serves 2
  • 4 all-beef hot dogs
  • 2 ears corn, husked
  • ⅓ cup mayonnaise
  • 2 oz cotija cheese, finely crumbled
  • 1 tsp chili powder or tajín
  • ½ tsp smoked paprika
  • 2 limes, halved
  • Small handful fresh cilantro, roughly chopped
  • Pinch of cayenne — optional but correct
Preparation
01Grill corn directly over high heat, turning every 2–3 minutes, until charred in spots on all sides — about 10 minutes. Set aside without wrapping. The char is the point.
02Score each hot dog with 4–5 diagonal cuts on each side, deep enough to open slightly when heated. This is not decorative. Surface area is where flavor lives.
03Grill scored dogs over high heat, turning until caramelized and the cuts have opened and charred at their edges — about 6 minutes total.
04While still hot, brush both the corn and the dogs generously with mayo. Roll in or dust heavily with cotija, chili powder, and smoked paprika.
05Squeeze lime aggressively — more than seems reasonable. Scatter cilantro. Add cayenne. Serve everything together on one platter at equal standing. Nobody in my flex spot earned equal standing last season but the food does.
My Note

Score the dogs. A round, unscored hot dog on a grill is a missed opportunity, and I have a well-documented talent for those. Four cuts per side minimum, and commit to the angle — a shallow cut closes back up under heat and gives you nothing. Preparation is the whole difference between a flex spot and a hole in my lineup, which took me eight months and a punishment week to work out.

The Commissioner's Board

Hot Honey Kielbasa · Whipped Feta · Accoutrements

Pairs with champagne, but you're not allowed to drink it, you're just allowed to watch someone else enjoy it

I made kielbasa lacquered enough to catch a reflection and put it next to whipped feta — hot honey, brown sugar, a board covered in cornichons and apple and crackers. Two servings felt right, because week sixteen and week seventeen came as a pair. Jordan, a man finishing 4-10, beat me by 0.22 points to secure my spot in the toilet bowl — my flex spot dropped 28.10 and it still wasn't enough, because the Seahawks D/ST went negative four and Deebo Samuel and Darren Waller barely showed up. Dane closed it out the following week by 37.84 and made it official. Somewhere between 0.22 and 37.84 is the exact point a season stops being unlucky and starts being earned.
ProteinKielbasa
MethodSkillet caramelize
VesselServing board
Time15 minutes active
Ingredients — Serves 2
  • 1 ring kielbasa (~14 oz), sliced into ½-inch coins
  • 2 tbsp hot honey
  • 1 tbsp brown sugar
  • 8 oz good feta, crumbled
  • 3 tbsp good olive oil
  • 1 tsp fresh lemon juice
  • 1 Honeycrisp apple, very thinly sliced
  • Cornichons, a generous handful
  • GF crackers ⚠️ verify label
  • Fresh thyme, optional
Preparation
01Make whipped feta first — ideally the day before: blend crumbled feta, olive oil, and lemon juice until completely smooth, 60–90 seconds. Adjust with olive oil for consistency. Refrigerate with a drizzle of oil on top. It improves overnight.
02In a skillet over medium-high, add kielbasa coins in a single uncrowded layer with no oil. Sear without touching 2–3 minutes until deeply browned. Flip and repeat.
03Reduce to medium. Add hot honey and brown sugar directly to the pan. Toss to coat. Cook 2–3 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the glaze darkens and clings. Watch carefully — it burns in under a minute at this stage.
04Transfer glazed kielbasa immediately to a board while the glaze is still molten. Arrange whipped feta in its bowl, apple slices, cornichons, and crackers in the remaining space. Garnish with thyme if using.
My Note

The kielbasa has to leave the pan and hit the board while the glaze is still hot and moving — wait too long and it seizes up, stuck to the pan instead of finished on the plate. I didn't move on fast enough either. One bad week became two before I'd even processed the first one.

The Golden Shower Dog

Bacon-Wrapped Dog · Chipotle Crema · Mango-Jalapeño Pico

Pairs with a hot shower and a great shower beer, washing away the season's losses while the rest of your life goes down the toilet right along with it

Modeled loosely after the trophy — golden, ceremonial, more about what it represents than what it tastes like — and best confronted the way you'd actually confront something like that: alone, in the shower, hot water running, crying a little, allegedly. Trevor commissioned that trophy with the deranged confidence of a man who knew the universe owed him one, then went and won the championship himself the very next year, like he was just collecting on a bet he'd placed against the rest of us. Karma's usually the excuse losers give themselves. His actually paid out. This is the last course. The punishment is over. I have made peace with none of it.
ProteinAll-beef dogs + bacon
MethodAir fryer, spiral-wrapped
VesselStandalone, plated
Time10 min prep · 12 min cook
Ingredients — Serves 2
  • 4 all-beef hot dogs
  • 8 strips thin-cut bacon — 2 per dog; thick-cut will not fully crisp in time
  • Toothpicks to secure each end
  • ½ cup sour cream or Mexican crema
  • 1–2 chipotles in adobo, minced + 1 tsp adobo sauce
  • 1 ripe mango, diced small
  • 1 jalapeño, seeded and minced
  • ¼ red onion, very finely diced
  • Juice of 1 lime
  • Small handful cilantro, chopped
  • Kosher salt
  • Edible gold leaf or gold dust, for garnish
Preparation
01Make the pico first: combine mango, jalapeño, red onion, lime juice, cilantro, and a pinch of salt. Let sit at minimum 10 minutes. The acid needs time to settle the raw onion, and some things need time to settle.
02Make the crema: stir together sour cream, minced chipotle, and adobo sauce until smooth. It should be smoky before it's spicy. Refrigerate until serving.
03Spiral-wrap each hot dog with 2 strips of thin-cut bacon starting at one end, overlapping slightly as you wind toward the other with no gaps. Secure both ends with toothpicks.
04Air fry at 400°F for 10–13 minutes, turning once at the halfway point, until the bacon is fully, completely, non-negotiably crisped on all surfaces. Press the underside to confirm. If it gives, it needs more time.
05Remove toothpicks. Spoon chipotle crema onto the plate first. Set dogs on top. Pile mango pico over each one. Serve immediately — the contrast between hot, crisp dog and cold, bright pico is the entire dish and it does not wait.
06Finish with a light dusting of edible gold just before serving — presentation over substance, same as the trophy this dish is named for.
My Note

Thin-cut bacon, and I'm not negotiating. Thick-cut gives you a soft exterior over an overcooked interior, and this roster already had enough of those. Week four I lost by 61.04 to the same man who'd go on to win it all — the earliest, clearest sign of how this was going to go, and I ignored it for thirteen more weeks. Cam Little was somehow the most reliable player I had for the back half of the season, which says less about him than it does about everyone else on this team. Cut your losses earlier than I did. That's the only lesson here that transfers to cooking.

Trevor built a golden hot dog trophy, named it "The Biggest Wiener," and the universe rewarded his audacity with a championship the very next season. McGlynn christened it first. I'm the second man in Devaraiders history to accept this trophy intimately, and I intend to handle it with more grace than I handled my roster.

Russell went 10-4 and still can't hold a relationship together for three weeks, which — coincidentally — is exactly how long Malik Nabers lasted on my roster before I paid $53 to watch him land on IR. JP is moving in with someone, which remains the most shocking development of the entire 2025 season, mine included. Jordan talked the most and won the least, a ratio that should be studied.

This fall, Claude runs the team. An AI with no emotional attachment to injured keepers, no loyalty to a DST that hasn't earned it, and no capacity to watch tape on a washed running back and think "one more year." If the Waivers of Rohan finish last again, it's the machine's fault and I'll have documentation. If we win, I'm taking full credit.

The hot dogs were better than they had any right to be. I cooked better this week than I managed my roster all season. I do not know what to do with that. The league does not care. The draft is in August.
