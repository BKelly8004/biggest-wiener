Nothing like going 6-8, barely missing the playoffs, and instead of quietly disappearing into the offseason, I fought my way to the toilet bowl to specifically finish dead last. I don't think I've ever worked that hard to fail so spectacularly, but at least I can tell my wife I don't have commitment issues when it comes to losing. Which brings us here.

My punishment: build an entire cookbook centered on phallic-shaped meat, which honestly should've gone to Russell given how much time he spends thinking about them. I got the trophy, he just got jealous.

I paid $53 to keep Malik Nabers, who lasted three weeks before his hamstring made better life decisions than I did all season. Still lasted longer than any of Russell's relationships, who somehow managed to go 10-4 this season, and even gave me my worst beating of the season in Week 11 with a whopping 108.78 to my sad 67.50. On the other hand, JP, a man so historically opposed to human connection that monks find him aloof, is now moving in with a woman, which is statistically more surprising than anything that happened in my lineup last season. And I even started Sam Darnold at quarterback, so the bar was already underground.

To give credit where it's due, McGlynn christened "The Biggest Wiener" last season: a gold trophy of a hot dog on a fork, mounted on a wooden base with a plaque that reads, without a trace of irony, "GLORIOUSLY LAST: FANTASY FOOTBALL'S FINEST." Trevor commissioned it with the deranged confidence of a man who knew the universe owed him one and decided to just win it all. I have no doubt McGlynn introduced it to his bootyhole in ways he's declined to elaborate on and the rest of us have declined to ask about. McGlynn then also went 10-4 this season. He also beat me in week one and again in week twelve, so the man who did that to the trophy still found time to do it to me twice. I am now the trophy's second intimate recipient. I have made my peace with this. I have also made an appointment.

The "wurst" part of this all is having to endure Jordan's incessant shit talking, which would land harder if he hadn't finished 4-10. And yet, I finished 6-8 and I'm the one cooking, all because he took me by 0.22 in the consolation bracket, which is the exact margin that man needs to never shut up again.

I've since decided to hand my roster management to Claude, because at some point the machine has to outperform the man who started the Saints D/ST for the better part of a season while they lost games in ways that felt personally targeted.

What follows is my sentence. Eight days. One protein, prepared seven filthy ways. I earned every bite by actively trying.

The Waiver Wire Special

Charred Whole Dog · Corn Tortilla · Chipotle Emulsion

ProteinAll-beef hot dogs
MethodGrill, crosshatch char
VesselGF corn tortillas
Time15 minutes
Ingredients — Serves 2
  • 4 all-beef hot dogs, left whole
  • 6 GF corn tortillas
  • ½ cup mayonnaise + 1–2 chipotles in adobo, blended smooth
  • 1½ cups green cabbage, very thinly shaved
  • ¼ cup pickled jalapeños
  • 2 oz cotija cheese, crumbled
  • 1 lime, cut into wedges
  • Neutral oil, kosher salt
Preparation
01Heat a grill or cast iron skillet over high heat until smoking. Add a thin film of neutral oil if using a pan.
02Score each hot dog in a tight crosshatch pattern, cutting about ⅛-inch deep on all sides. This lets the fat render out and the surface curl and crisp as it cooks.
03Grill whole, turning every 60–90 seconds, until deeply charred and the scored cuts have opened up and blistered — about 6–8 minutes total.
04Warm tortillas directly over a gas flame or dry pan until lightly charred at the edges.
05Spread chipotle mayo on each tortilla. Lay one whole charred dog in each. Top with cabbage, jalapeños, cotija. Finish with an aggressive squeeze of lime and flaky salt.
