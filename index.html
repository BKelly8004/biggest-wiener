The Waiver Wire Special

Smashed All-Beef Dog · Corn Tortilla · Chipotle Emulsion

I smashed a hot dog flat against screaming cast iron because sometimes the only way to get something to perform is to apply pressure it never consented to — a philosophy I discovered too late applies to fantasy rosters, not just meat. This dog showed up off the waiver wire and outworked guys I paid real money for, which is the most backhanded compliment I can give a piece of processed pork. Nick Westbrook-Ikhine, 25% owned, started in my flex out of sheer desperation, quietly did more for my team in week fourteen than Malik Nabers did on my bench all season with 1,200 receiving yards and zero say in the matter.
ProteinAll-beef hot dogs
MethodCast iron smash
VesselGF corn tortillas
Time15 minutes
Ingredients — Serves 2
  • 4 all-beef hot dogs, split lengthwise
  • 6 GF corn tortillas
  • ½ cup mayonnaise + 1–2 chipotles in adobo, blended smooth
  • 1½ cups green cabbage, very thinly shaved
  • ¼ cup pickled jalapeños
  • 2 oz cotija cheese, crumbled
  • 1 lime, cut into wedges
  • Neutral oil, kosher salt
Preparation
01Heat a cast iron skillet over high heat until smoking. Add a thin film of neutral oil.
02Place hot dogs cut-side down. Smash firmly with a heavy spatula and hold for 30 seconds. Do not touch them again. Let the crust build undisturbed for 2–3 minutes until deeply mahogany — darker than feels right.
03Flip and sear the skin side 60 seconds. Rest on a wire rack, not paper towels. The crust deserves air.
04Warm tortillas directly over a gas flame or dry pan until lightly charred at the edges.
05Spread chipotle mayo on each tortilla. Place one smashed dog half per taco. Top with cabbage, jalapeños, cotija. Finish with an aggressive squeeze of lime and flaky salt.
My Note

Press harder than feels appropriate. A hot dog that isn't being firmly handled is just lying there doing nothing — much like my roster management from weeks one through thirteen. Commit to the press. The crust does not form from hesitation.

Pigs in a Playoff Blanket

Upside-Down Puff Pastry Dog · Gruyère · Everything Crust

Wrapped in pastry, flipped upside down, baked until the cheese underneath crisps into something lacquered and completely disconnected from what's actually inside. This dog made the postseason. I did not. It's currently handling that better than I am. I had Malik Nabers, Trey McBride, and Marvin Harrison Jr. on my roster and still watched the playoffs from my couch with the Saints D/ST somehow still active in my starting lineup, which at this point felt less like a decision and more like a hostage situation I'd created for myself.
ProteinAll-beef hot dogs
MethodOven bake, inversion
VesselGF puff pastry
Time10 min + 20 min oven
Ingredients — Serves 2
  • 4 all-beef hot dogs
  • 1 sheet GF puff pastry, thawed — verify label; Pepperidge Farm is NOT GF
  • 4 slices Gruyère or Swiss, cut to fit
  • 1 egg, beaten
  • 2 tbsp everything bagel seasoning
  • Whole grain mustard, for serving
  • Parchment paper
Preparation
01Preheat oven to 400°F. Line a baking sheet with parchment and lightly grease it.
02Lay cheese slices directly on the parchment. Place one hot dog centered on top of each.
03Cut puff pastry into strips wide enough to wrap each dog with slight overlap. Drape over, tucking edges beneath. Brush generously with egg wash. Press everything bagel seasoning firmly into the surface.
04Bake 18–22 minutes until deeply golden and the cheese has crisped and caramelized at the visible edges. The color should make you slightly nervous before you pull it.
05Rest exactly 3 minutes. Slide a thin spatula fully underneath and flip cheese-side up onto a board. Serve immediately with whole grain mustard.
My Note

I dropped CJ Stroud for Sam Darnold mid-season with the quiet confidence of a man who has learned nothing. The pastry flip requires the same energy — commitment, no hesitation, accepting what's underneath. Unlike the Stroud decision, this one worked out.

Tartare of Last Resort

All-Beef Hot Dog · Capers · Dijon · Shallot · Raw Egg Yolk

Finely diced, plated with a raw yolk on top like it's trying to seduce someone at a wedding. It's a hot dog wearing a tuxedo it stole. Ring mold, capers, shallot, dijon, the whole classical French treatment applied to $53 worth of processed pork that lasted exactly as long as Malik Nabers did on my roster — three weeks, no explanation, gone before anyone looked too closely. I served it with a straight face and zero regrets, the same energy I brought to every start/sit decision that ruined my season.
ProteinAll-beef hot dogs
MethodNo-cook · knife work
VesselRing mold, plated
Time15 minutes
Ingredients — Serves 2
  • 4 all-beef hot dogs, very finely diced — knife work only, no food processor
  • 2 tbsp shallot, minced
  • 1 tbsp capers, roughly chopped
  • 1 tsp dijon mustard
  • 1 tsp Worcestershire sauce ⚠️ GF (Lea & Perrins is GF)
  • ½ tsp hot sauce
  • 2 raw egg yolks
  • Cornichons and GF crackers or cucumber rounds, to serve
  • Flaky salt, black pepper, chives to finish
Preparation
01Dice hot dogs as finely as possible — small, uniform pieces. This is not the time for rustic. Treat it like it deserves to be here.
02In a bowl, combine diced hot dog, shallot, capers, dijon, Worcestershire, and hot sauce. Mix gently until just combined. Season with salt and pepper. Taste. Adjust.
03Press mixture firmly into a ring mold on each plate. Smooth the top. Carefully unmold.
04Nestle one raw egg yolk into a small indent pressed into the top of each tartare. It should sit there with quiet confidence.
05Finish with chives and flaky salt. Arrange cornichons and crackers or cucumber alongside. Serve immediately and accept the compliments without revealing what it is until after the first bite.
My Note

The knife work is the entire point. A food processor turns this into paste and the illusion collapses entirely — much like my season once people looked at the actual standings. Fine dice. Steady hand. Committed presentation. The confidence has to come from somewhere since the ingredient isn't providing it.

Fourth and Wellington

Hot Dog Wellington · Mushroom Duxelles · Prosciutto · GF Puff Pastry

I went for it on fourth down all year and it never worked — until I wrapped a hot dog in mushrooms, ham, and pastry and somehow produced the single best decision of my entire season. Nothing else I touched in 2025 deserved this much effort. This dog didn't need it and got it anyway. Life's unfair like that. The crust shatters at the touch and the inside is exactly what it promised to be, which puts it one for one against my starting lineup all year.
ProteinAll-beef hot dogs
MethodDuxelles wrap, oven roast
VesselGF puff pastry
Time45 min prep · 25 min oven
Ingredients — Serves 2
  • 4 all-beef hot dogs, patted very dry
  • 8 oz cremini or baby bella mushrooms, very finely chopped
  • 2 tbsp butter
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tsp fresh thyme leaves
  • 4 slices prosciutto, laid flat
  • 1 sheet GF puff pastry, thawed ⚠️ verify GF label
  • 1 egg, beaten (egg wash)
  • Dijon mustard, for brushing
  • Flaky salt, black pepper
  • Whole grain mustard, to serve
Preparation
01Make the duxelles: melt butter in a skillet over medium-high heat. Add mushrooms and cook, stirring often, until all moisture has evaporated and the mixture is dark, dry, and deeply concentrated — about 12–15 minutes. Add garlic and thyme for the final 2 minutes. Season and cool completely.
02Brush each hot dog lightly with dijon. Lay a sheet of plastic wrap flat. Arrange 2 prosciutto slices overlapping in a rectangle. Spread a thin, even layer of cooled duxelles over the prosciutto. Place a hot dog at the edge and roll tightly using the plastic wrap to help. Twist the ends to seal. Refrigerate 15 minutes minimum.
03Preheat oven to 425°F. Cut puff pastry into rectangles large enough to wrap each prosciutto-wrapped dog. Remove plastic wrap, place the roll at the pastry edge, roll tightly, and seal the seam and ends with egg wash. Brush the entire surface with egg wash.
04Score the pastry top lightly with a knife in a crosshatch or diagonal pattern. Sprinkle with flaky salt. Place seam-side down on a parchment-lined baking sheet.
05Bake 20–25 minutes until the pastry is deeply golden and shatteringly crisp. Rest 5 minutes before slicing. Serve with whole grain mustard and the quiet satisfaction of having done something correctly for once.
My Note

The duxelles must be completely dry before you use them. Any residual moisture turns the pastry soggy from the inside — a wet, collapsed crust that promised everything and delivered nothing. I have been on the receiving end of that exact experience from multiple roster decisions last season. Dry the mushrooms. Take your time. Do it right.

The Consolation Brat

Bratwurst · Sun-Dried Tomato Cream · GF Rigatoni

The sauce alone has ended engagements in the good direction. Add a hard-seared brat and rigatoni and you've got something richer and more satisfying than any conversation I had with my bench all season. Sun-dried tomatoes, heavy cream, garlic, parmesan, one pan, twenty-five minutes — this is what commitment looks like. I wouldn't know personally, but the pasta does. I had Nabers, Harrison, McBride, and Montgomery on my roster and somehow still finished with the fewest points in the league, so clearly having good ingredients was never really my problem.
ProteinBratwurst
MethodHard sear, one-pan sauce
VesselGF rigatoni or penne
Time25 minutes
Ingredients — Serves 2
  • 4 bratwurst links
  • 12 oz GF rigatoni
  • 4 cloves garlic, thinly sliced
  • ½ cup sun-dried tomatoes in oil, roughly chopped
  • 1 cup heavy cream
  • ½ cup parmesan, finely grated, plus more to finish
  • 1 tsp red pepper flakes
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • Fresh basil, to finish
  • Olive oil, salt, black pepper
Preparation
01Bring a large pot of heavily salted water to boil. Cook pasta to al dente. Reserve one full cup of pasta water before draining.
02In a wide skillet over medium-high, sear brats whole with no oil. Turn every 2 minutes until deeply browned on all sides, about 8–10 minutes. Remove, rest briefly, slice on the bias into ¾-inch rounds.
03Reduce to medium. Add a splash of olive oil to the same pan — do not wipe it, those browned bits are the whole point. Sauté garlic 60 seconds. Add sun-dried tomatoes, red pepper flakes, oregano. Stir 1 minute.
04Pour in cream. Simmer 3–4 minutes until slightly thickened. Add parmesan and stir until melted. Season aggressively.
05Return sliced brats to the sauce. Add drained pasta and toss over medium heat, adding pasta water in small splashes until the sauce is glossy and coats every ridge. Serve with extra parmesan and torn basil.
My Note

A pale, unseared brat sliding around in cream sauce is a waste of everyone's time — and I say this as a man who started Courtland Sutton while Malik Nabers posted historic numbers on my bench. Sear it until you're uncomfortable. Then go one more minute. That's where the season should have been managed from the start.

The Flex Spot

Grilled All-Beef Dog · Full Elote Treatment · Charred Corn

Scored, charred, and dressed up in mayo, cotija, and lime like it's trying to impress someone's parents. Meanwhile my actual flex spot spent all season starting guys with four catches and a prayer. The corn on the side has more consistency than anyone I rostered in week fourteen, when I started a tight end who caught for four total yards and lost me the game by four points. I scored the dogs diagonally and thought about that decision the entire time I was grilling, which is either growth or the beginning of a very specific kind of psychosis.
ProteinAll-beef hot dogs
MethodGrill or cast iron char
VesselStandalone — no bun
Time20 minutes
Ingredients — Serves 2
  • 4 all-beef hot dogs
  • 2 ears corn, husked
  • ⅓ cup mayonnaise
  • 2 oz cotija cheese, finely crumbled
  • 1 tsp chili powder or tajín
  • ½ tsp smoked paprika
  • 2 limes, halved
  • Small handful fresh cilantro, roughly chopped
  • Pinch of cayenne — optional but correct
Preparation
01Grill corn directly over high heat, turning every 2–3 minutes, until charred in spots on all sides — about 10 minutes. Set aside without wrapping. The char is the point.
02Score each hot dog with 4–5 diagonal cuts on each side, deep enough to open slightly when heated. This is not decorative. Surface area is where flavor lives.
03Grill scored dogs over high heat, turning until caramelized and the cuts have opened and charred at their edges — about 6 minutes total.
04While still hot, brush both the corn and the dogs generously with mayo. Roll in or dust heavily with cotija, chili powder, and smoked paprika.
05Squeeze lime aggressively — more than seems reasonable. Scatter cilantro. Add cayenne. Serve everything together on one platter at equal standing. Nobody in my flex spot earned equal standing last season but the food does.
My Note

Score the dogs. A round, unscored hot dog on a grill is a missed opportunity, and I have a well-documented talent for those. Four cuts per side, minimum. Commit to the angle. The flex spot rewards preparation, which is something I apparently needed eight months and a punishment week to internalize.

The Commissioner's Board

Hot Honey Kielbasa · Whipped Feta · Accoutrements

Trevor built a gold penis on a fork, handed it to a man who deserved it, and then won the championship the very next year like the universe owed him one. I made kielbasa lacquered enough to catch a reflection and put it next to whipped feta, because if Trevor gets to live like royalty off a trophy shaped like a dick, the least I can do is eat like it too. Hot honey, brown sugar, a board covered in cornichons and apple and crackers — this is what winning looks like. I wouldn't know from experience this year, but I can at least eat like I do.
ProteinKielbasa
MethodSkillet caramelize
VesselServing board
Time15 minutes active
Ingredients — Serves 2
  • 1 ring kielbasa (~14 oz), sliced into ½-inch coins
  • 2 tbsp hot honey
  • 1 tbsp brown sugar
  • 8 oz good feta, crumbled
  • 3 tbsp good olive oil
  • 1 tsp fresh lemon juice
  • 1 Honeycrisp apple, very thinly sliced
  • Cornichons, a generous handful
  • GF crackers ⚠️ verify label
  • Fresh thyme, optional
Preparation
01Make whipped feta first — ideally the day before: blend crumbled feta, olive oil, and lemon juice until completely smooth, 60–90 seconds. Adjust with olive oil for consistency. Refrigerate with a drizzle of oil on top. It improves overnight.
02In a skillet over medium-high, add kielbasa coins in a single uncrowded layer with no oil. Sear without touching 2–3 minutes until deeply browned. Flip and repeat.
03Reduce to medium. Add hot honey and brown sugar directly to the pan. Toss to coat. Cook 2–3 minutes, stirring occasionally, until the glaze darkens and clings. Watch carefully — it burns in under a minute at this stage.
04Transfer glazed kielbasa immediately to a board while the glaze is still molten. Arrange whipped feta in its bowl, apple slices, cornichons, and crackers in the remaining space. Garnish with thyme if using.
My Note

The kielbasa must leave the pan and hit the board while the glaze is still hot and moving. A glaze that cools in the pan loses everything — it goes from lacquered and alive to stuck and dull. I held onto the Saints D/ST all season past the point where any of its warmth was still useful. The kielbasa deserved better judgment than my lineup did.

The Dead Cap

Bacon-Wrapped Dog · Chipotle Crema · Mango-Jalapeño Pico

Wrapped tight in bacon with zero gaps — coverage I never once managed on my actual roster. $53 for three weeks of Malik Nabers still haunts me the way missed field goals haunted my kicker's entire season. This is the last course. It's spicy, it's sweet, and unlike my 2025 campaign, it actually sticks the landing. The punishment is over. I have made peace with none of it. Claude takes over in the fall, and I genuinely believe a language model has better roster instincts than I do at this point, which is not a flattering sentence to type out loud.
ProteinAll-beef dogs + bacon
MethodAir fryer, spiral-wrapped
VesselStandalone, plated
Time10 min prep · 12 min cook
Ingredients — Serves 2
  • 4 all-beef hot dogs
  • 8 strips thin-cut bacon — 2 per dog; thick-cut will not fully crisp in time
  • Toothpicks to secure each end
  • ½ cup sour cream or Mexican crema
  • 1–2 chipotles in adobo, minced + 1 tsp adobo sauce
  • 1 ripe mango, diced small
  • 1 jalapeño, seeded and minced
  • ¼ red onion, very finely diced
  • Juice of 1 lime
  • Small handful cilantro, chopped
  • Kosher salt
Preparation
01Make the pico first: combine mango, jalapeño, red onion, lime juice, cilantro, and a pinch of salt. Let sit at minimum 10 minutes. The acid needs time to settle the raw onion, and some things need time to settle.
02Make the crema: stir together sour cream, minced chipotle, and adobo sauce until smooth. It should be smoky before it's spicy. Refrigerate until serving.
03Spiral-wrap each hot dog with 2 strips of thin-cut bacon starting at one end, overlapping slightly as you wind toward the other with no gaps. Secure both ends with toothpicks.
04Air fry at 400°F for 10–13 minutes, turning once at the halfway point, until the bacon is fully, completely, non-negotiably crisped on all surfaces. Press the underside to confirm. If it gives, it needs more time.
05Remove toothpicks. Spoon chipotle crema onto the plate first. Set dogs on top. Pile mango pico over each one. Serve immediately — the contrast between hot, crisp dog and cold, bright pico is the entire dish and it does not wait.
My Note

Thin-cut bacon. Non-negotiable. Thick-cut produces a soft exterior on an overcooked interior, which is exactly what Jake Moody gave me all season — missed ten field goals, still started every week, still somehow my problem. Use thin-cut. Cut your losses earlier than I did. These are the two lessons this season taught me and only one of them applies to cooking.
